 Diet can have a significant impact on an individual's risk of developing heart disease, the leading cause of death globally. Consuming a diet that is high in certain components can increase the risk of heart disease, while a diet that is rich in nutrients and low in added sugars can help lower that risk. Here are some key ways in which diet can affect heart health:

1. Saturated and trans fats: Consuming high amounts of saturated and trans fats can increase the levels of low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol in the blood, which can lead to the buildup of plaque in the arteries and increase the risk of heart disease. These fats are found in high-fat dairy products, fatty cuts of meat, and processed and fried foods.
2. Sugars: Consuming high amounts of added sugars can increase triglyceride levels and blood pressure, both of which are risk factors for heart disease. Limit your consumption of added sugars found in sweets, baked goods, and sugary beverages.
3. Fiber: A high-fiber diet can help lower cholesterol levels, regulate blood sugar, and promote healthy digestion. Fiber can be found in whole grains, fruits, vegetables, legumes, and nuts.
